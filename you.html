And You!

Your choices matter more than you think.

What can I do?

Every single day you make choices — what to eat, how to get to school, what to buy, and what to throw away. Each of those choices has an impact on the environment. On their own they might seem tiny, but millions of people making better choices every day adds up to something massive. The best part? You do not need to be an adult or have lots of money to start.

How?

Smart Choices!

There are lots of small things you can do right now that make a real difference.

Reduce food waste — only take what you will actually eat. Food that ends up in landfill rots and releases methane, a gas that is 25 times more damaging to the planet than car exhaust fumes. Poor waste control is responsible for 30% of the methane that enters our atmosphere.

Use the Three R's — Reduce what you use in the first place, Reuse things before throwing them away, and Recycle whatever you can so it becomes something new instead of ending up in landfill. In the UK, 56% of waste still ends up in landfill because it is not recycled.

Buy second hand — giving something a second life with a new owner is much better for the planet than buying new. It also saves money. Places like Facebook Marketplace are great for finding hidden gems.

Repair instead of replace — before buying a new phone or gadget, ask whether it actually needs replacing or whether it can be fixed. Electronic waste is a growing problem caused by throwing away devices that still work perfectly well.

Why is it not always possible?

Making eco-friendly choices is not always easy, and that is not always your fault.

Cost is a big barrier. Durable, long-lasting products are often much more expensive than cheap alternatives. If a family does not have a lot of money to spare, buying the cheaper option is sometimes the only realistic choice, even if it is worse for the planet. Sustainable food, ethical clothing, and energy-efficient home upgrades all cost more upfront.

Not everything is accessible. Cycling to school sounds great, but it is not realistic for everyone — some people live too far away, roads are not always safe, and some people have disabilities that make it impossible.

Green jobs and skills take time. The world is slowly shifting to cleaner industries, but that transition is not instant. When old industries close, like coal mining, the communities that depended on those jobs can be left behind — even if the change is good for the environment overall.

The point is not to be perfect, it is to make better choices where you can, and to understand why it is sometimes complicated for others.
